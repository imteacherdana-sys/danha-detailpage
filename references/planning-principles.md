# Planning Principles

Use these principles to preserve the Danah-style detail-page planning direction while producing ecommerce-style image cuts.

## Strategy Before Design

Before writing cut copy, identify:

- Who is most likely to buy this product.
- What problem, desire, anxiety, or occasion brings them to the page.
- What must be proven before they can buy.
- Which facts are verified and which are missing.
- What the first 3 cuts must make the buyer feel or understand.
- What the final 3 cuts must resolve before purchase.

## Persuasion Flow

Prefer this flow unless the category requires another order:

1. Immediate buying context or campaign signal.
2. Social proof or review proof.
3. Problem empathy.
4. Product promise.
5. Differentiators and benefits.
6. Proof through detail, material, ingredient, process, usage, or comparison.
7. Anxiety removal: options, delivery, cautions, FAQ, return, storage.
8. Final CTA.
9. Product information disclosure.

For Coupang, the first two cuts and final disclosure position are fixed by `SKILL.md`.

## Copy Rules

- Write short mobile-readable Korean headlines.
- Use concrete sensory words, buyer situations, and visible benefits.
- Separate facts from assumptions.
- Do not show internal labels like AIDA, PAS, TRUST, or ACTION to the user.
- Keep claims legally safe, especially for cosmetics, food, health supplements, baby, pet, medical, and household chemical products.

## Review-Led Planning

When reviews exist, use them to discover:

- The customer's own words.
- Repeated satisfaction points.
- Unexpected usage moments.
- Purchase anxieties that disappeared after use.
- Phrases that can become headline or USP candidates.

Do not turn reviews into stronger claims than they actually support.

## Product Photo Planning

For each product image, decide:

- Hero use, review cut use, detail proof, texture, package, option, size, lifestyle, or disclosure.
- Whether the original photo is usable as-is, needs cleanup, or should be used only as reference.
- Where text can safely sit without covering labels or key product details.

Final image generation must preserve the real product appearance when photos are provided.
